The audit, five answers

1. Planning or resources? Both, but the real limit is flow, not tokens.

The weekly plan is at 20% used (Lead, 20:4x IST). Tokens are not short. What is short is things that only one session can do, and builders working at the same time.

  • One reader for everything. Every pipeline PR waits for the tech lead to read its diff (the read gate). That cost 29.5 h today, the largest single loss. Waits: median 25 min, worst 212 min. The Lead changed 8 times today (Lead 1 to Lead 8), and each hand-over restarts the reading.
  • One lane in the queue for pipeline work. The pipeline scope of the merge queue (Mergify) takes one batch at a time, about 32 min each, and was 91% busy from 11:30 to 18:00. Main-fix PRs had no way to jump the line: 17.4 h.
  • An optional check let a broken change in. guards (the pipeline's own self-tests) is not a required check, so #3924 (private-records split) merged with a red part and broke main for 2 h 55 min. Together with two other stacked causes: 13.3 h of red main.
  • Planning gap: under-used builders. The triage page (§3) says 7 bundles (A1, A3 to A8) can run at once on separate files once #3833 (one file per guard rule, ends the hot-file clash) lands. Today about 5 cloud lab lanes ran in the morning (STATE, 11:37 IST). The four must-have PRs of bundle 1 had no builder from the account switch until 20:3x IST, when the founder started the new "Lab bundle 1" lane; it has already merged main into #3814 and #3825. Ten Mac sessions today were Leads or standby Leads; none were builders.
  • Planning gap: the target was wrong. The 23:00 plan counted all 137 register rows. Only 27 are must-haves for the restart (class A). That was corrected at 18:2x (P-852).

Sources: lab-replan-2026-11-27-flow.md (measured 18:10 IST), triage §3, session list read 20:4x IST.

  • Whole pipeline machinery on main today: 49,470 lines. Tools folder 44,687 lines in 313 files (182 shell, 77 Python), of which 108 self-test scripts are 11,029 lines; 29 GitHub workflows 3,491 lines (whole .github folder); merge-queue file 543 lines (42 scopes, 4 queue rules); .claude 708 lines (12 skills, 2 agents, settings, 15 rule files); git hooks 41 lines.   • Product app for scale: 163,826 lines (api 76,491, web 40,066, database schema and migrations 28,852, engine 14,687, contracts 3,730), plus 180,373 lines of tests and 13,331 of acceptance checks.

Against common practice (DORA lead-time research, trunk-based development, merge queues with required checks, code-owner review, flaky-test quarantine): our parts match the practice. The amount does not. A team's delivery tooling is usually a small slice of its code; ours is 30% of the app. Over-engineered: 161 rules, 108 scripts that test the tools, mutation tests of the pipeline itself, 29 workflows, reports that block nobody. Under-engineered where it counts: a slow check left optional (guards), no priority lane for fixes, failure logs cut to their last 20 lines, and a human-style read on every pipeline PR where practice uses code owners plus an automated approver (the planned switch, A10).
