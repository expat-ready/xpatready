* **A cross-firm line has no firm, and `AuditEntry.firmId` is required.** `ports/audit.ts` `AuditEntry.firmId` is a uuid, not nullable, so `AuditPort.record` cannot write the `platform` chain's `cross_tenant.access` line. **Ruled (Lead 61, comment 6900882697 on #1875):** one additive change, `firmId: FirmId.nullable()`, null allowed only for `OUR_STAFF` and `SYSTEM` lines on the `platform` chain (the adapter refuses it for any other actor type), with one contract case; this row changes `ports/audit.ts` (on part 1's touch list). `ports/audit.ts` is shared with AUD-1b and AUD-1m: in series.
* **Our staff's actor word.** `AuditActor` allows `staff:`, `system:`, `seed:`; AUD-1a maps `staff:*` to `FIRM_STAFF` only when `actor_type` is left empty, and AUD-1b's adapter takes `actor_type` from the context first. Recommended: our staff write `staff:<our staff id>` with `actor_type = OUR_STAFF` from the context, and every reader branches on `actor_type`, never on the word (no change to the port's word rule or to AUD-1a's function).
* **SA-11-31's report holds cross-firm operations too.** SA-11-31@feb1029c: "the audit list filtered to that firm's impersonations and cross-tenant operations; strictly firm-scoped". The row's report reads only the firm's own `OUR_STAFF` lines; a cross-firm read lives on the `platform` chain. So every `cross_tenant.access` line names the firms it touched in flat fields (`AuditDetail` allows scalars and arrays of at most 50 scalars, strings at most 200 characters): `scope` (the route name, a string), `filterFirmId` (when the read was filtered to one firm), `firmIds` (the firms in the answer, when at most 50) or `allFirms: true` (when more, or when unfiltered), `rowCount`. The report includes the `platform` lines in its date range where `detail->>'filterFirmId'` is the firm, or `detail->'firmIds' ? firm`, or `detail->>'allFirms' = 'true'`; nothing else of the `platform` chain. Those lines stay hidden from the firm itself (AUD-4).
* **An export's line is committed before its data, never held open with it.** Writing `audit.exported` and then streaming in one transaction would hold AUD-1c's per-chain lock for the whole download, and with fail-closed audit (P-607 (3)) every action of that firm would wait. As in AUD-4: open a read-only `REPEATABLE READ` snapshot, count the rows, write and **commit** the export's lines in their own short transaction (on the firm's chain, or `platform`), then stream from the snapshot (which cannot see the export's own line or anything after it); a failed line means no file.
* **"No route can forget" is enforced at run time, fail-closed.** P-607 (3) ("no action without its audit line") applies: an interceptor on `/api/system/*` refuses to send a route's answer that wrote no access line (500, code `AUDIT_LINE_MISSING`, one `audit_write_failed` alert), and the start-up sweep proves every `/api/system/*` route is behind it. A test proves both.
