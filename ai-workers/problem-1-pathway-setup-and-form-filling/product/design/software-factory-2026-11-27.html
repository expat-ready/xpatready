Account switch

  1. Trigger. The active individual account's weekly meter nears its limit.
  2. Close side. At 94% the Lead's check runs close-prep: STATE and RESUME pushed, every lane pushes its work.
  3. New side. A SessionStart hook detects the new account, prints the checklist and starts blocked until the minute-0 reading is taken.
  4. Ledger. One row per switch.

Being built register row LR-143.

Gaps found while drawing this
  • Team-organisation server-managed settings (the one-model rule, LR-145) reach only sessions signed in to the Team organisation. Our lanes run on individual accounts, so the repository's own hooks and settings remain the enforcement that reaches every account.
  • The harness's small fast model (the /goal evaluator and helpers) is not Opus today: a founder decision is owed.
  • The private split moves records only after every reader is re-pointed (PR #3911 plan).

Life of one change

flowchart LR
  A[Row shaped by the Lead] --> B[Builder: branch + draft PR]
  B --> C[Fast checks + affected suites on the PR]
  C --> D[Reviewer agent: Approve exact commit]
  D --> E{Money, schema,\npermissions?}
  E -- yes --> F[Lead reads the diff]
  E -- no --> G[Label: review approved]
  F --> G
  G --> H[Mergify batch of 2:\nfull 24-part test run]
  H -- green --> I[Merged to main]
  H -- red --> J[Dequeued: fix or cover,\nfull run, relabel]
  J --> G
  I --> K[Demo updates]
  I --> L[qa proof on main]
  L --> M[Capability: Works]
  1. Shaped. The Lead makes a row ready: a duplicate check, the files it may touch, its acceptance test, a "Done when" line naming that test, and every founder decision already answered. New screens also need a concept, an independent challenge and an approval.
  2. Allowed to start. waves.py admits work only from the open wave, and caps work in progress (8 building, 12 built but not yet proven).
  3. Built. A fresh branch from main, a draft pull request, every step pushed. Under 800 changed lines unless a decision allows more.
  4. Reviewed. The reviewer agent (and the breaker for risky changes) checks the exact commit and posts Reviewer: Approve <commit>. At most two rounds; then the Lead decides.
  5. Labelled. review: approved goes on only with that Approve and green checks. A gate adds it automatically for ordinary changes; the label guard removes it the moment a new commit is pushed.
  6. Queued and merged. Mergify takes two approved PRs at a time, runs all 24 test parts on them together with main, and merges only on green. A red batch sends the PRs back ("dequeued").
  7. Demo moves. The demo on the Mac (port 4400) updates itself to the new main.
  8. Proven. qa runs the capability's test pack on main, a second independent run confirms it, and only then is it "Works". An open Major bug keeps it "Partial".
  9. Recorded. The Lead updates STATE (where we are), BACKLOG (what is next) and DECISIONS (append only).

How we measure

  • Works: capabilities proven by qa, against a target of 10 a day (counted 07:00 to 07:00 IST).
  • Flow: time from Approve to merge (target under 1 hour), batch failure rate (under 10%), test time thrown away (under 20%), records share of merges (under 20%). Tool: flow-measure.py.
  • The constraint: every 30-minute check names the one stage that holds the most work (lead-watch.sh).
  • DORA: the industry standard now has five measures (deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, failed deployment recovery time, and deployment rework rate, added this year). Our weekly CTO review still uses four; it moves to five.
